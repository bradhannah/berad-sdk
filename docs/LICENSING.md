# Licensing and commercial use

## SDK-LIC-001 — MIT project source and documentation

Project-authored SDK code and documentation are distributed under the
[MIT license](../LICENSE). It permits commercial use, modification, redistribution,
and sale, including use within proprietary/closed-source games. It does not require
publishing a consumer game's source or paying royalties. Retain the copyright and
permission notice as the license requires; the warranty disclaimer also applies.

Preserve existing copyright/license notices when transferring source. New repository
ownership or a new top-level license must not erase previous authors' notices.
Each published module MUST carry the applicable license and attribution files so
its independently downloaded archive contains the required notices.

## SDK-LIC-002 — Third-party code, media, and fixtures

Dependencies, fonts, recordings, images, and fixtures retain their own terms.
A game's source-code license does not automatically cover its media. Before adding
third-party material, record origin, license, notices, redistribution conditions,
and suitability for the intended commercial SDK use. Unknown provenance is not
permission; unresolved material remains outside the redistributable module.

Prefer generated synthetic assets for examples/tests, or explicitly licensed
fixtures whose conditions are documented and included. Do not give the SDK an
implicit dependency on a game's production media or original installation.
Applications may supply their own assets under their own terms.

The foundation includes no production game media. Automated checks can verify
declared files and notices; they cannot independently establish copyright ownership
or redistribution rights.
